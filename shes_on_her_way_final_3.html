Urban Legend

She's On Her Way

"It didn't want to hurt her. It just wanted to learn what she was."

This was sent to me by someone who knew one of the girls. I don't know them personally. The footage is real as far as I can tell. I'm posting it because I think people in the area should know.

Location
Ypsilanti, Michigan
Date
October 2010
Time
Friday Night
I The house before everyone arrives 8:11 PM

Riley is seventeen and alone and happy about it — at least for now. Her parents left that morning for an anniversary weekend. Back Sunday. She has the house to herself for the first time and she's been planning this sleepover for two weeks.

Three friends coming: Dana, Cass, Morgan. She's already in pajamas, hair up, padding around in socks. Sleeping bags on the living room floor — she laid them out herself, one two three, neat, heads toward the TV. Snacks half-organized on the kitchen counter. Reality TV on in the background, sound low. Music from her laptop. She's humming.

At some point she goes to the back door — opens it, leans out to check if the porch light works, clicks it on and off twice. Satisfied. Comes back inside. Does not lock the door behind her. Doesn't think about it.

She goes to the front door, opens it, leans on the frame looking at the quiet street. Presses the doorbell — it sounds weak, she frowns, presses it again. Makes a mental note. Goes back inside. Locks the front door. Picks up her phone.

II The doorbell 8:29 PM

The doorbell rings. Riley lights up — runs to the front door, swings it open, big smile ready.

Nobody there.

She steps onto the porch. Looks left. Looks right. Empty street. Parked cars. A dog barks somewhere far away and stops. The porch light makes its small circle and beyond it everything is just dark.

She laughs, slightly unsure, goes back inside.

III What's in the backyard 8:44 PM

Riley is back in the kitchen. Sitting on the counter, swinging her legs, eating from the snack bag, scrolling her phone. Cass texts that traffic is bad, maybe twenty more minutes. Riley sighs, sets the phone down, stretches.

Through the window above the sink — in the dark backyard, near the back fence — something is there.

It is doing what Riley was just doing.

Standing, but bent. Limbs arranged at the angles her body was making thirty seconds ago when she was sitting on the counter. Arms at her exact position. Head tilted the same way. Not threatening. A copy. Produced a beat too late by something that was watching closely enough to remember.

Riley does not look at the window.

She gets up, walks to the fridge, gets a drink. The shape in the backyard slowly straightens back to standing — over about ten seconds, the way a recording plays back at the wrong speed.

Her laptop in the next room skips forward three tracks on its own.

She calls out: "Hello?"

Checks. Nothing. Resets the playlist. Comes back to the kitchen.

The chair at the kitchen table is pulled out from the table by about a foot.

She notices it. Pushes it back in. Frowns. Keeps moving.

—

Riley calls Morgan directly. Four rings. Morgan picks up, mid-conversation with someone in the car: "Hello? Riley? What's up—"

"Were you just texting me?"

"No I've been driving. Why, what happened?"

Riley pulls the phone from her ear. Looks at the number in the call. Looks at the number in the thread.

One digit is different.

The contact showed as Morgan because it was close enough that her phone filled in the name from her recents. It is not Morgan's number. She has never seen it before.

Morgan's voice in the phone: "Riley? Hello? You're scaring me."

"How far away are you?"

"Like ten minutes. Are you okay?"

"Just come in when you get here."

She hangs up. Looks at the front door.

Locks it.

—
V The fourth sleeping bag 9:06 PM

Every light in the house is on. TV louder. Riley is on the couch, knees pulled up, phone in hand. Waiting. Trying to feel normal.

She looks at the living room floor. The sleeping bags she laid out. She laid out three. She counted them herself — one two three — neat, heads toward the TV.

There are four.

The fourth is at the end of the row. Same direction as the others. Same spacing. Same careful alignment. It was not thrown down. It was placed — the same way she placed the others.

She gets up. Counts again. Four. She doesn't touch the fourth one. She backs away until the couch hits the backs of her legs. Sits down. Stares at it.

Her phone buzzes.

VI The friends arrive 9:08 PM

Dana, Cass, and Morgan pile through the front door with bags and snacks and noise. Warm, real, chaotic. The house fills up. Cass has her camera out already — she films everything, always has. Morgan is immediately in the snacks. Dana hugs Riley and Riley holds on a beat longer than usual.

Riley pulls Dana aside. Tells her everything. The doorbell. The texts. The number. The chair. Dana listens with wide eyes.

Cass starts doing a ghost investigation bit — deep narrator voice, panning her camera around dramatically — and everyone laughs, and the tension breaks, and it genuinely feels okay.

Cass pans her camera across the living room floor in her ghost voice: "And here we have the alleged haunted sleeping situation—"

She stops.

Looks at the floor. Counts.

"Riley why are there four sleeping bags."

Not a question. The ghost voice is gone.

All four of them look at the sleeping bags. Then at each other.

Morgan, quietly: "There are only three of us."

—
VII The mirror — the corner — the handprint 9:41 PM

Riley's room. Door locked. All four of them inside, close together on the bed and floor. Cass is still filming — nervous habit, camera as shield.

Cass pans slowly around the room. Window — closed, blinds down. Closet — open, just clothes. The door. The mirror on the back of the door, full-length, showing the whole room back at them.

In the reflection: four girls. Riley, Dana, Morgan, Cass.

And in the reflected corner behind them — the corner that the mirror shows but that you can't see without turning around — something is standing.

Tall. Still. The face not quite visible. Mouth resting open. Pupils too dark and too wide for the light in the room.

It is wearing Riley's posture from earlier in the night — the happy posture, the waiting-for-her-friends posture. Arms at her side, weight shifted to one hip, head tilted slightly right. The exact position she held when she was alone in the kitchen, texting, content.

A memory of her. Worn by something else.

Cass keeps filming. Keeps talking. Pans away.

Riley: "Cass. Go back."

Cass pans back to the mirror.

The corner of the reflection is empty.

All four of them stare at the mirror. At the empty reflected corner. At nothing.

Then — from the actual corner behind them, the one none of them are facing — the sound of something that has been standing very still shifting its weight. One movement. The specific sound of a presence adjusting.

They all turn around at the same time.

The corner is empty.

But on the wall — on the bare paint at shoulder height — a handprint. Pressed flat, fingers together, palm full against the surface. Not dried. Warm. The way you press your hand to a wall when you have been standing in one place for a very long time and needed something to hold onto.

Riley reaches out and touches the edge of it without thinking. Pulls her hand back.

From directly outside the locked bedroom door: a doorbell sound. Once. Coming from inside the house. From the wrong side of the wall entirely.

The door handle moves. Slowly. Tested. Holds against the lock. Releases.

Silence.

Then — pressed against the door from outside, just audible — the sound of a palm laying flat against wood.

A pause.

Then again. Slightly different pressure.

Then again.

Learning the motion. Not knocking yet. Practicing.

Nobody moves.

Riley's phone on the bed buzzes. All four of them look at it.
